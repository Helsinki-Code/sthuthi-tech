## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Sthuthi Technologies trains and certifies company employees on trending AI tools — Claude Code, Codex, Microsoft Copilot, Gemini, Cursor, or a custom internal tool — with a proctored assessment and a verifiable credential.

**What it does:** Runs instructor-led (and async) cohort training on a specific AI tool, then proctors a task-based, in-tool assessment reviewed by a human assessor. A pass issues a dated certificate with a unique credential ID that an employer or client can verify. For tools that don't have a published track, Sthuthi scopes and builds a custom curriculum and rubric directly with the client's engineering leads.

**Product category:** Enterprise AI-tool training & certification (a credentialing body for workplace AI-tool competency — adjacent to corporate L&D / technical upskilling, not a general online-course platform).

**Product type:** B2B service — cohort-based training + proctored assessment + issued credential, sold to companies for their employees (not direct-to-consumer).

**Business model:** Enterprise engagements booked per cohort (seat-based), plus scoped custom-certification projects priced/timelined per engagement. No self-serve public pricing on the site; buyers go through a scoping call.

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** Organisations whose employees actively use (or are being rolled out onto) AI coding/productivity tools — engineering-heavy companies, agentic-AI product teams, and enterprises standardising a specific AI tool across a department. Early named clients are themselves AI/agentic-AI and fintech companies (see Proof Points).

**Decision-makers:** Engineering leadership (CTOs, platform/tech leads), L&D and People Ops leaders, and whoever owns AI-tool rollout decisions inside the company.

**Primary use case:** Proving — not assuming — that employees can competently and safely use a specific AI tool at work, with a credential that can be checked by HR systems or hiring managers.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Standardise how a whole team uses a given AI tool (so usage isn't ad hoc per engineer)
- Get a defensible, checkable record that named employees are competent on a specific tool, for hiring, promotion, or client-facing credibility
- Certify a proprietary/internal AI tool or agent framework that no public vendor certification covers

**Use cases:**
- Onboarding a new hire onto a Copilot-enabled codebase with a shared standard
- Certifying a platform team on Claude Code before they're allowed to run it against production repos
- Building a bespoke certification around an internal agent framework under NDA

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Companies roll out AI coding/productivity tools faster than they can verify anyone is actually competent — or safe — with them.

**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Vendor "certifications" are often just course-completion certificates with no proctored assessment
- Generic AI-literacy training isn't tool-specific, so it doesn't test the actual judgment calls (diff review, context selection, when to hand off vs. take over) that matter for a given tool
- Internal/proprietary tools have no certification path at all

**What it costs them:** Ungoverned or inconsistent AI-tool usage across engineering teams; no defensible record for hiring, client trust, or internal promotion decisions; re-litigating "can this person actually use this tool" every time it matters.

**Emotional tension:** Leadership doesn't want to bet a client relationship or a production repo on assumed AI fluency that's never been tested.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Vendor-issued "certificates" for individual tools (e.g., a Copilot completion badge) — fall short because they test course completion, not applied judgment, and aren't proctored.
**Secondary:** Generic corporate AI-literacy / prompt-engineering training platforms — fall short because they're tool-agnostic and don't assess inside the real tool on a real task.
**Indirect:** Doing nothing / trusting self-reported AI fluency — falls short because it leaves no record and no verification path.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Task-based assessment run inside the actual tool (not a multiple-choice quiz about it)
- Every attempt gets a named human assessor sign-off, not an automated pass/fail
- A credential ID + verification record an employer or client can check directly
- Willing and equipped to build fully custom tracks around internal/proprietary tools under NDA
- Tracks are leveled (Foundations → Practitioner → Expert) so a new hire and a five-year engineer aren't trained identically

**How we do it differently:** Cohort training capped at ~20 seats, capped-length live sessions, plus async modules, ending in a closed-book, in-tool practical assessment reviewed by a person.

**Why that's better:** The credential reflects demonstrated capability on real tasks, not attendance — so it's actually usable as a hiring, staffing, or client-trust signal.

**Why customers choose us:** They need a specific tool's competency verified (not generic AI training), or they need a certification built around a tool that has no public vendor track at all.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "We can just use the vendor's own certification." | Most vendor certifications aren't proctored or task-based — ours is assessed inside the real tool with a human reviewer, and we cover tools with no vendor certification path at all. |
| "Our internal tool isn't something anyone certifies." | That's the custom-track model: we scope it with your engineering leads and build the curriculum and rubric around your actual tool, under NDA if needed. |
| "How do we know the credential means anything?" | Every certificate carries a unique credential ID and a named assessor; pass criteria are agreed with the client's engineering leads before a cohort runs, and pass rates aren't optimised for. |

**Anti-persona:** Companies looking for a fast, low-friction "AI literacy" badge for marketing purposes rather than a real, assessed competency record — Sthuthi explicitly is not optimising for high pass rates or minimal friction.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Leadership can no longer justify assuming AI-tool competency when repos, clients, or compliance are on the line.
**Pull:** A proctored, tool-specific assessment with a verifiable credential ID — plus the option to certify tools with no existing vendor path.
**Habit:** Defaulting to whatever completion certificate a tool vendor already offers, or doing no formal certification at all.
**Anxiety:** Whether a proctored assessment will disrupt delivery schedules, and whether pass/fail outcomes will be seen as punitive rather than developmental.
